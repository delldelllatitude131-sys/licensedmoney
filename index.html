Section 01

What the Ministry of Law Registry of Moneylenders Is and How to Use It

The foundation of safe borrowing in Singapore rests on one authoritative source: the Registry of Moneylenders Singapore, managed by the Ministry of Law (MinLaw). This is the only official record of all entities legally permitted to lend money in the country.

The Registry publishes a downloadable PDF list of all licensed moneylenders, updated regularly. As of early 2026, there are approximately 153 licensed moneylenders operating in Singapore. Any lender not on this list is operating illegally.

This verification process takes less than five minutes but provides an essential layer of protection against impersonation scams.

Section 02

Step-by-Step Guide to Verifying a Licensed Moneylender's Status Online

Beyond the Registry, additional verification steps help confirm the lender is not only licensed but also operating within the law.

🏢

Physical Office Verification

Visit during business hours. The licence certificate must be displayed prominently on-premises and match the Registry licence number.

🪪

Document Handling

A legitimate lender will never retain your NRIC, passport, work permit, or ATM card. Documents must be returned immediately after checking.

📵

Communication Channels

Licensed lenders are prohibited from sending unsolicited loan ads via SMS, WhatsApp, calls, or social media. All legitimate ads are through their own websites or physical premises.

Loan Contract Clarity

You must receive a complete Note of Contract that includes the exact loan amount, interest rate with calculation method, full repayment schedule, all fees, and the total repayment amount. Never sign a blank or incomplete contract.

3

Face-to-Face Verification Requirement

Licensed moneylenders must conduct in-person identity verification before granting any loan. You must visit their approved place of business. A loan processed entirely online or via messaging apps is not legal.

4

Credit Check Practices

Licensed lenders must perform a credit check via the Moneylenders Credit Bureau (MLCB) or Credit Bureau Singapore (CBS). They will request your consent and explain how your credit history affects approval. No credit check = a serious red flag.

5

Borrowing Limits Compliance

6

Customer Reviews and Reputation

Check Google Reviews and independent financial comparison sites. Look for patterns: hidden fees, aggressive collection, or poor communication are legitimate concerns. Note how the lender responds to negative feedback — a professional response is a positive sign.

7

Branch Presence and Accessibility

A licensed moneylender must operate from approved premises. A verifiable, staffed physical office is not a convenience — it is a regulatory requirement. If you cannot find an address on the Registry, the lender is not legitimate.

Section 05

Red Flags That Signal an Unlicensed or Fraudulent Lender

Recognising warning signs is just as important as knowing what to look for. If you encounter any of the following, walk away immediately.

📱
Unsolicited Contact

Licensed moneylenders cannot cold-call, text, or message you on social media. Any unsolicited offer promising "instant approval," "no credit check," or "guaranteed loans" is from an unlicensed operator.

🔑
Requests for Singpass Credentials

No legitimate lender will ever ask for your Singpass ID and password. This is used by scammers to access your CPF accounts or commit identity fraud.

⚠️
Pressure Tactics and Threats

Licensed lenders are prohibited from abusive language, threats, or harassment. Any pressure to sign quickly or threats of legal action without proper process are serious violations.

💸
Upfront Fees Before Disbursement

Scammers ask for "processing fees," "GST fees," or "insurance fees" before releasing a loan. Legitimate lenders only charge an admin fee upon approval — never before you receive funds.

🚫
No Physical Office or Refusal to Meet

If a lender insists everything can be done online or refuses an in-person meeting, they are not a licensed moneylender. The in-person verification requirement is non-negotiable under Singapore law.

⚠️ Important: Borrowing from a loan shark is not just financially dangerous—it is also an offence under Singapore law. Those who knowingly borrow from unlicensed moneylenders can face legal consequences.

Section 07

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I check a lender's licence online?

Yes. The Ministry of Law maintains the official Registry of Moneylenders on its website. You can download the latest list at any time to verify a lender's status, licence number, and approved office address.

What if I suspect a lender is unlicensed?

Report them immediately to the Registry of Moneylenders with as much detail as possible. You can also contact the Police hotline at 1800-255-0000 or the X-Ah Long hotline at 1800-924-5664.

What documents do I need to apply?

Singapore Citizens and PRs typically need their NRIC and proof of income (payslips or CPF statements). Foreigners need their passport, valid work permit, proof of residence, and income documentation. Licensed lenders may also request a credit report from the Moneylenders Credit Bureau.

Can I borrow from multiple licensed moneylenders at once?

Yes, but your total borrowing across all licensed moneylenders cannot exceed the legal limits based on your income. Licensed lenders are required to check your existing loans through the Moneylenders Credit Bureau before approving new credit.

What happens if I cannot make a repayment?

Contact your lender as early as possible. Licensed moneylenders must follow proper procedures and may discuss alternative arrangements. For financial distress, Credit Counselling Singapore provides free advice and assistance. Ignoring the issue leads to late fees and potential legal action.
